# Academic Workflow Protocols

Read only the section relevant to the selected skill and mode.

## Literature review

1. Convert the question into searchable concepts, synonyms, alloy/design variants, methods, and outcome terms.
2. Record databases, exact query concepts, dates, filters, and language limits.
3. Deduplicate candidates and screen titles/abstracts, then full text when accessible.
4. Record inclusion and exclusion decisions. Distinguish primary research, reviews, and secondary/grey literature.
5. Verify bibliographic identity before relying on a source.
6. Extract results into the shared evidence matrix, including method, conditions, limitations, and claim locator.
7. Synthesize by theme or mechanism; show contradictions and method-dependent explanations.
8. End with evidence-bounded gaps, limitations, and the date/search boundary.

For a systematic review, define the protocol and eligibility rules before screening, preserve counts at every step, assess risk of bias with a method appropriate to study type, and explain whether quantitative synthesis is valid.

## Research-gap analysis

1. State the candidate gap narrowly enough to falsify.
2. Map established findings and their source quality.
3. Map direct counterexamples, adjacent work, and reviews that claim broader coverage.
4. Identify the exact missing link: condition, material state, method, time scale, mechanism, comparator, or outcome.
5. Explain why the missing link matters and what observation would close it.
6. Report search limits and grade the gap as `well-supported`, `provisional`, or `not established`.

Do not turn novelty language in a paper into independent proof of a gap. Treat author-stated gaps as claims to verify.

## Evidence verification

1. Split complex prose into atomic claims.
2. For each cited source, verify existence and metadata independently from claim support.
3. Locate the relevant source passage, figure, table, result, or abstract statement.
4. Compare the manuscript claim's direction, magnitude, population/material, conditions, and uncertainty to the source.
5. Record `VERIFIED`, `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`, `UNVERIFIED`, or `CONTRADICTED` with reasoning.
6. Search for corrections, retractions, version changes, and stronger contradictory evidence where relevant.
7. Return an audit trail and required fixes. Do not rewrite prose during the audit.

## Manuscript review

1. Freeze the review copy and identify the review standard.
2. Assess contribution and fit separately from scientific validity.
3. Anchor every major finding to manuscript evidence.
4. Review methodology, analysis, reporting, source use, claim strength, and reproducibility.
5. Test alternative explanations and inspect whether limitations bound the conclusions.
6. Preserve role-separated findings before synthesis when simulating multiple perspectives.
7. Give categorical findings, minimum remedies, and a reasoned recommendation; never use invented precision.
8. Keep the report separate from the manuscript.

## Full pipeline gates

- Before drafting: verified evidence matrix exists and unsupported planned claims are labeled.
- Before review: manuscript consistency and citation audit are recorded.
- Before revision: every reviewer concern has a stable identifier and author disposition.
- After revision: changed claims and citations are reverified from the revised artifact.
- Before finalization: unresolved integrity issues and limits are visible to the user.
